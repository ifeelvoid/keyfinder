KeyFinder 2.1 — macOS 12 or later, Intel and Apple Silicon

KeyFinder is an independent release without an Apple Developer ID, so it is not
notarized and macOS asks you to confirm it the first time. You only do this once.

1. APP
   Drag KeyFinder.app onto the Applications shortcut.
   Open KeyFinder from Applications. If macOS says it cannot verify the developer:
     - macOS 15 Sequoia or later: open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll to
       Security, click "Open Anyway" next to KeyFinder, then confirm with your password.
     - macOS 12-14: Control-click KeyFinder.app, choose Open, then click Open.

2. PLUG-INS (Ableton, Logic, FL Studio, etc.)
   KeyFinder appears as an audio effect / analyzer, not an instrument.
   Open Finder, press Shift-Command-G, and go to:  ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins
   Copy  Plug-Ins/VST3/KeyFinderVST.vst3       into the VST3 folder.
   Copy  Plug-Ins/AU/KeyFinderVST.component    into the Components folder (Logic, GarageBand).
   Restart your DAW and rescan plug-ins.

   If your DAW skips the plug-in or says it is damaged, open Terminal and run:
     xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/KeyFinderVST.vst3
     xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/KeyFinderVST.component
   This only clears the download flag on these two files; it does not change macOS security.

   Upgrading from 2.0: the 2.0 plug-in was registered as an instrument. Re-add KeyFinder
   to your tracks as an effect after updating.

Your audio files are only read, never modified.
